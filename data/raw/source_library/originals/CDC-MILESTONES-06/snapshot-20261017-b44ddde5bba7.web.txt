L35: cite13†Image: baby grabbing for a toy L36: ## Ways to monitor milestones
L37: 
L38: How your child plays, learns, speaks, acts, and moves offers important clues about your child's development. Developmental milestones are things most children (75% or more) can do by a certain age.

L79: ## Share with your child’s doctor
L80: ### Things to share and consider
L81: 
L82:   * What are some things you and your baby do together?
L83:   * What are some things your baby likes to do?
L84:   * Is there anything your baby does or does not do that concerns you?
L85:   * Has your baby lost any skills he/she once had?
L86:   * Does your baby have any special healthcare needs or was he/she born prematurely?
L87: ### If you or the doctor are still concerned
L88: 
L89:   * Ask for a referral to a specialist who can evaluate your child more; and
L90:   * Call your state or territory's early intervention program to find out if your child can get services to help. Learn more and find the number at cite30†cdc.gov/FindEI .

L94: ## Tips and activities
L95: ### What You Can Do for Your 6-month-old
L96: 
L97: cite32†Image: dad reading a book to his daughter in his lap L98: 
L99: As your baby’s first teacher, you can help his or her development.
L100: 
L101: As your baby's first teacher, you can help his or her learning and brain development. Try these simple tips and activities in a safe way. Talk with your baby's doctor and teachers if you have questions or for more ideas on how to help your baby's development.
L102:   * Use "back and forth" play with your baby. When your baby smiles, you smile; when he makes sounds, you copy them. This helps him learn to be social.
L103:   * "Read" to your baby every day by looking at colorful pictures in magazines or books and talk about them. Respond to her when she babbles and "reads" too. For example, if she makes sounds, say "Yes, that's the doggy!"
L104:   * Point out new things to your baby and name them. For example, when on a walk, point out cars, trees, and animals.
L105: ### More Parenting Tips and Activities
L106:   * Sing to your baby and play music. This will help his brain develop.
L107:   * Limit screen time (TV, tablets, phones, etc.) to video calling with loved ones. Screen time is not recommended for children younger than 2 years of age. Babies learn by talking, playing, and interacting with others.
L108:   * When your baby looks at something, point to it and talk about it.
L109:   * Put your baby on her tummy or back and put toys just out of reach. Encourage her to roll over to reach the toys.
L110:   * Learn to read your baby's moods. If he's happy, keep doing what you are doing. If he's upset, take a break and comfort your baby.
L111:   * Talk with your baby's doctor about when to start solid foods and what foods are choking risks. Breast milk or formula is still the most important source of "food" for your baby.
L112:   * Learn when your baby is hungry or full. Pointing to foods, opening his mouth to a spoon, or getting excited when seeing food are signs that he is hungry. Others, like pushing food away, closing his mouth, or turning his head away from food tells you that he's had enough.
L113:   * Help your baby learn she can calm down. Talk softly, hold, rock, or sing to her, or let her suck on her fingers or a pacifier. You may offer a favorite toy or stuffed animal while you hold or rock her.
L114:   * Hold your baby up while she sits. Let her look around and give her toys to look at while she learns to balance herself.
L115:   * Hold your baby with both of your hands securely under her arms and let her stand. Look around with your baby and name what you can see while she is standing.
L116:   * Introduce your baby to different sounds and voices. For example, whisper, clap, or make funny noises. See if your baby looks or is curious and see which sounds she prefers.
L117:   * Playing on the floor or play mat with your baby every day helps him to move, learn, and explore.
L118:   * When he drops a toy on the floor, pick it up and give it back. This game helps him learn cause and effect.
