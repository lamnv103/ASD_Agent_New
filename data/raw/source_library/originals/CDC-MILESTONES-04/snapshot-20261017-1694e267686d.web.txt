L35: cite13†Image: 4 month old baby L36: ## Ways to monitor milestones
L37: 
L38: How your child plays, learns, speaks, acts, and moves offers important clues about your child's development. Developmental milestones are things most children (75% or more) can do by a certain age.

L79: ## Share with your child’s doctor
L80: ### Things to share and consider
L81: 
L82:   * What are some things you and your baby do together?
L83:   * What are some things your baby likes to do?
L84:   * Is there anything your baby does or does not do that concerns you?
L85:   * Has your baby lost any skills he/she once had?
L86:   * Does your baby have any special healthcare needs or was he/she born prematurely?
L87: You know your child best. Don't wait. If your child is not meeting one or more milestones, has lost skills he or she once had, or you have other concerns, act early. Talk with your child's doctor, share your concerns, and ask about developmental screening.
L88: ### If you or the doctor are still concerned
L89: 
L90:   * Ask for a referral to a specialist who can evaluate your child more; and
L91:   * Call your state or territory's early intervention program to find out if your child can get services to help. Learn more and find the number at cite31†cdc.gov/FindEI .

L95: ## Tips and activities
L96: ### What you can do for your 4-month-old
L97: 
L98: cite33†Image: Baby holding toy with her parents L99: 
L100: As your baby’s first teacher, you can help his or her development.
L101: 
L102: As your baby's first teacher, you can help his or her learning and brain development. Try these simple tips and activities in a safe way. Talk with your baby's doctor and teachers if you have questions or for more ideas on how to help your baby's development.
L103:   * Respond positively to your baby. Act excited, smile, and talk to him when he makes sounds. This teaches him to take turns "talking" back and forth in conversation.
L104:   * Provide safe opportunities for your baby to reach for toys, kick at toys and explore what is around her. For example, put her on a blanket with safe toys.
L105:   * Allow your baby to put safe things in his mouth to explore them. This is how babies learn. For example, let him see, hear, and touch things that are not sharp, hot, or small enough to choke on.
L106: ### More Parenting Tips and Activities
L107:   * Talk, read, and sing to your baby. This will help her learn to speak and understand words later.
L108:   * Limit screen time (TV, phones, tablets, etc.) to video calling with loved ones. Screen time is not recommended for children younger than 2 years of age. Babies learn by talking, playing, and interacting with others.
L109:   * Feed only breast milk or formula to your baby. Babies are not ready for other foods, water or other drinks for about the first 6 months of life.
L110:   * Give your baby safe toys to play with that are easy to hold, like rattles or cloth books with colorful pictures for her age.
L111:   * Let your baby have time to move and interact with people and objects throughout the day. Try not to keep your baby in swings, strollers, or bouncy seats for too long.
L112:   * Set steady routines for sleeping and feeding.
L113:   * Lay your baby on her back and show her a bright-colored toy. Move the toy slowly from left to right and up and down to see if she watches how the toy moves.
L114:   * Sing and talk to your baby as you help her "exercise" (move her body) for a few minutes. Gently bend and move her arms and legs up and down.
L115:   * Play with your baby by holding him securely under his arms with his feet on your lap. Let him bounce up and down.
L116:   * Play games, such as peek-a-boo. You can cover your eyes with your hands and then uncover and say "peek-a-boo." Watch for your baby's smile or other signs he is enjoying the game.
L117:   * Pay close attention to how your baby reacts to different things. This will help you learn what she likes and doesn't like and what makes her feel good.
L118:   * Copy your baby's sounds and see how long your baby "talks" back and forth with you.
L119:   * Call your baby by his name to help him learn it. He will start to recognize it around 9 months. For example, "Are you ticklish, Jordan?"
L120:   * Playing on the floor or play mat with your baby every day helps him to move, learn, and explore.
L121:   * Hold and talk to your baby; smile and be cheerful while you do.
L122:   * Have quiet play times when you "read" or sing to your baby.
