L23: cite12†Español L24: # Signs and Symptoms of Autism Spectrum Disorder
L25: ## Key points
L26:   * Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a developmental disability caused by differences in the brain.
L27:   * People with ASD often have problems with social communication and interaction, and restricted or repetitive behaviors or interests.
L28:   * People with ASD may also have different ways of learning, moving, or paying attention.
L29:   * It is important to note that some people without ASD might also have some of these symptoms. But for people with ASD, these characteristics can make life very challenging.
L30: cite13†Image: A mother helping her son doing homework in kitchen L31: ## Signs and symptoms
L32: ### Social communication and interaction skills
L33: 
L34: Social communication and interaction skills can be challenging for people with ASD.
L35: 
L36: Examples of social communication and social interaction characteristics related to ASD can include
L37:   * Avoids or does not keep eye contact
L38:   * Does not respond to name by 9 months of age
L39:   * Does not show facial expressions such as happy, sad, angry, and surprised by 9 months of age
L40:   * Does not play simple interactive games like pat-a-cake by 12 months of age
L41:   * Uses few or no gestures by 12 months of age (for example, does not wave goodbye)
L42:   * Does not share interests with others by 15 months of age (for example, shows you an object that they like)
L43:   * Does not point to show you something interesting by 18 months of age
L44:   * Does not notice when others are hurt or upset by 24 months (2 years) of age
L45:   * Does not notice other children and join them in play by 36 months (3 years) of age
L46:   * Does not pretend to be something else, like a teacher or superhero, during play by 48 months (4 years) of age
L47:   * Does not sing, dance, or act for you by 60 months (5 years) of age
L48: Keep Reading: cite5†Screening for Autism Spectrum Disorder L49: ### Restricted or repetitive behaviors or interests
L50: 
L51: People with ASD have behaviors or interests that can seem unusual. These behaviors or interests set ASD apart from conditions defined by problems with social communication and interaction only.
L52: 
L53: Examples of restricted or repetitive behaviors and interests related to ASD can include
L54:   * Lines up toys or other objects and gets upset when order is changed
L55:   * Repeats words or phrases over and over (called echolalia)
L56:   * Plays with toys the same way every time
L57:   * Is focused on parts of objects (for example, wheels)
L58:   * Gets upset by minor changes
L59:   * Has obsessive interests
L60:   * Must follow certain routines
L61:   * Flaps hands, rocks body, or spins self in circles
L62:   * Has unusual reactions to the way things sound, smell, taste, look, or feel
L63: cite14†Image: child playing with blocks by stacking them L64: 
L65: Contact your child's doctor if you have any concerns about your child's development.
L66: ### Other characteristics
L67: 
L68: Most people with ASD have other related characteristics. These might include
L69: 
L70:   * Delayed language skills
L71:   * Delayed movement skills
L72:   * Delayed cognitive or learning skills
L73:   * Hyperactive, impulsive, and/or inattentive behavior
L74:   * Epilepsy or seizure disorder
L75:   * Unusual eating and sleeping habits
L76:   * Gastrointestinal issues (for example, constipation)
L77:   * Unusual mood or emotional reactions
L78:   * Anxiety, stress, or excessive worry
L79:   * Lack of fear or more fear than expected
L80: It is important to note that children with ASD may not have all or any of the behaviors listed as examples here.
